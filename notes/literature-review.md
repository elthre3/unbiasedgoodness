# Goodness-of-fit testing of a data-selected linear model: literature review and a publishable direction

Date: 2026-09-26. Companion files: `evidence-table.csv` (66 records, one per source, with how each was verified) and `search-log.tsv` (every API query, page fetch and web search, with timestamps).

Verification standard. Every source in the evidence table was resolved to a DOI or arXiv identifier and its bibliographic record confirmed from Crossref, Semantic Scholar or the arXiv abstract page. Sources marked "full text read" were read by a delegated reader working only from the fetched arXiv text under a quote-and-locate protocol; the quotes that carry the argument below were then re-located by me in the extracted texts (the `verified_how` column says which). Sources marked "metadata only" or "abstract" were not read and are cited only for existence and topic. Nothing below rests on recall. Journal venues are given only where Crossref or the arXiv page confirmed them; where only Semantic Scholar's record supports a venue, the table says so.

## 1. The question, stated precisely

Data: y = mu + eps, eps ~ N(0, sigma^2 I_n), fixed design X (n x p), p > n allowed. A path algorithm (forward stepwise with groups and a penalised stopping rule, or the cross-validated lasso) selects M0 from y, and the same path is continued to an enlargement M1 with M0 subset of M1 and |M1| about ceiling(m|M0| + b). The package tests

H0(M0): mu in span(1, X_M0)   against   H1(M0, M1): mu in span(1, X_M1),

and asks for a test whose level is controlled conditionally on the selection event {M0-hat = M0, M1-hat = M1} (or on a finer event implying it), with sigma unknown. The package's stored simulations (n = 100, p = 200, five true nonzero coefficients, Toeplitz(1/3) design) show:

| test | rejection at 5%, selected model contains the truth | rejection, signal missed |
|---|---|---|
| selective F conditional on the full path plus stopping-rule signs | 0.035 (se 0.008) | 0.047 |
| naive F | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| RP test on lasso-added variables | 0.83 to 0.90 | not applicable |
| RP test on all unselected variables | 0.000 to 0.006 | 0.000 to 0.006 |

So the only valid test has power indistinguishable from its size, and the two residual-prediction variants are respectively invalid and powerless. The review below explains all four numbers from the literature and identifies what is not yet known.

## 2. What the literature establishes, and how strongly

### 2.1 Why the RP-on-lasso-added test is invalid (strong, mechanism verified)

Shah and Bühlmann (2018, JRSS-B) define the residual prediction test's null as the sparse Gaussian linear model in all p columns; their group test is for a fixed group G, and in their simulations "we took Gc to be a randomly selected set of size p/2 containing S". Post-selection use of a data-chosen G is not discussed anywhere in the paper. Janková, Shah, Bühlmann and Samworth (2020, JRSS-B) give the mechanism: the direction w must come from an "independent auxiliary sample (e.g. derived through sample splitting)" because otherwise "w [is] strongly dependent on R even under the null; as such calibration ... would be problematic". The lasso-added variables are exactly a direction chosen for its correlation with the residuals, so the 0.83 to 0.90 rejection rate is the expected failure, not a bug. Battey, García Rasines and Tang (2025, arXiv:2507.10373) state the same phenomenon for likelihood-ratio tests of a candidate model against a lasso-reduced set: such a test "typically rejects too often in hypothetical repeated use".

### 2.2 Why the RP-on-all-unselected test is powerless (strong)

That test is valid because its null is the full sparse model, which is the Shah and Bühlmann null. Verzelen and Villers (2010, AoS; arXiv:0711.2119) give the minimax separation rates for testing a linear subspace against added variables: k log(p)/n for k-sparse departures, sqrt(p)/n for dense ones, and the Fisher test on all added variables is rate-optimal only for k > sqrt(p) and n > (1 + gamma)p. A dense statistic over about 190 unselected columns when the departure is at most one or two coefficients is in the wrong regime by a factor of order sqrt(p)/(k log p). Shah and Bühlmann's own simulations say the same: RP tests beat the F test "most notably when the alternative is sparse", while for "small effects spread out across many variables ... the global test tends to do best".

### 2.3 Why the fully conditioned selective F test has no power (strong, but the F-specific theorem does not exist)

The package conditions on the whole ordered path through step M, on the direction/nuisance statistics (Loftus and Taylor's z, v_Delta, v_2), and on the signs of the stopping-criterion differences. Four independent lines of evidence say this is where the power goes.

- Information accounting. Fithian, Sun and Taylor (arXiv:1410.2597, eq. 21): the expected information lost by conditioning on the selection variable S equals the information S carries about the parameter; "we will typically sacrifice power as we move from coarser to finer selection variables" (Prop. 3). Loftus and Taylor (2015) acknowledge this for their own construction: "Conditioning on (z, v_Delta, v_2) reduces power, but it is unclear how to compute the truncation region without using this strategy", and "Optimizing computation to make increased power feasible by not conditioning on z is an area of ongoing work". Their Prop. 6.1 shows the truncation limits are set by neighbouring path statistics and that "a close lower limit ... results in low power".
- Boundedness of the truncation set. Kivaranovic and Leeb (2021, JASA 116(534):845-857), Prop. 1: for a truncated normal on T, "If T is bounded either from above or from below, then E[U(W) - L(W)] = infinity". Their Remark 3 says the result applies to any selection event that is a finite union of polyhedra and points to the truncated t and F cases without analysing them. The rejection rate of 0.035 under the alternative is the test-side symptom of the same geometry.
- Saturated versus selected model. Fithian, Taylor, Tibshirani and Tibshirani (arXiv:1512.02565) show, in their Example 1 with mu = (0, 4), that the saturated-model test of poor fit has "power alpha = 0.05" while the selected-model test "rejects about half of the time ... successfully detecting the poor fit of M0"; in their Table 3 the probability of reaching the true model size is 0.04 with saturated-model ForwardStop versus 0.66 with max-z. Fithian, Sun and Taylor say directly (p. 23): "The selected-model approach is especially well-suited for testing goodness of fit of a selected linear model". The package's F test is a saturated-model construction (it conditions on z).
- Path conditioning. Tibshirani, Taylor, Lockhart and Tibshirani (2016, JASA), Sec. 4.7: "a greater degree of conditioning will generally lead to less powerful tests and wider intervals"; in their Sec. 6.1 the spacing test, which conditions on less, has about 82% power at step 2 versus 35% for the exact path-conditioned test, at a nominal 10% level. Sugiyama, Le Duy and Takeuchi (2021) find that conditioning on history and signs "significantly decreases the power" and describe the cross-validation version in Loftus (2015) as "highly over-conditioned".

What does not exist: a theorem about the power of a conditional F or chi test of nested selected models. Kivaranovic and Leeb treat intervals for a linear contrast; Azaïs, de Castro and Mourareau (2018, Bernoulli) give the only formal power analysis of a sequential selective test I found, and it is for the global-null spacing test. Neufeld, Perry and Witten's 2026 review (arXiv:2604.09779) says the conditional goal can "virtually eliminate all power" but gives no F-test result.

### 2.4 Which remedies have proven gains (strong for intervals and single contrasts; nothing for F tests)

- Randomised selection. Tian and Taylor (2018, AoS), Lemma 5: with Gaussian randomisation of variance gamma^2 the "leftover Fisher information is bounded below by (1 - tau) I(theta), tau = sigma^2/(sigma^2 + gamma^2)", and "When there is no randomization gamma = 0, we potentially have no leftover Fisher information". Kivaranovic and Leeb (2024, EJS 18(1)), Thm 3.1: randomised or carved selection plus the polyhedral method gives interval length bounded by sigma (Phi^{-1}(a2) - Phi^{-1}(a1)) sqrt(1 + sigma^2/tau^2), and "sacrificing some power in the model-selection step results in an significant increase in power in subsequent inferences". Fithian, Sun and Taylor's Sec. 7 simulation, in the package's own regime (n = 100, p = 200, 7-sparse), reports conditional power 0.80 with no data held out, 0.97 when 25% is held out and carved, 0.47 for splitting at the same fraction, and "Carve98 and Carve99 have much higher power than Carve100: 91%, 86%, and 80%".
- (U, V) splitting and fission. García Rasines and Young (2023, Biometrika): U = Y + W for selection, V = Y - W/gamma for inference, exact when sigma is known; Thm 1 gives asymptotic validity with a sigma-hat computed from the first n/2 observations for convex selection events. Their Table 4 (n = 200, p = 30, 90% intervals, f = 3/4, rho = 0.5) has length 0.899 for splitting versus 0.644 for the randomised version; the text's phrase "roughly 75% shorter" is inconsistent with the table ratios (23% to 29%), so I report the table. Leiner, Duan, Wasserman and Ramdas (2025, JASA) give the same construction as fission with a = 1/(1 + tau^2) equating the information split, and note that with unknown variance one must "condition on both f(Y) and sigma-hat, which may not have a tractable distribution in high dimensional settings". Neither paper discusses goodness of fit.
- Conditioning on less. Lee et al. (2016, AoS): conditioning on the model only is "more efficient (i.e., narrower intervals, more powerful tests), at the price of more computation", but 2^|M| sign patterns are "not feasible when we select hundreds of variables". Liu, Markovic and Tibshirani (arXiv:1801.09037) give a grid recipe along the test direction for "any blackbox model selection procedure" and report intervals "roughly half as long". Sugiyama et al. (2021) do it for stepwise selection with an AIC stopping rule, whose history event is "characterized by a finite number of quadratic inequalities of z" (Lemma 2). Shiraishi, Miwa, Le Duy and Takeuchi (2024, JJSDS 7(2):633-665) give anytime bounds on such p-values and, in their Sec. 4.1, a selective chi test for a group in forward stepwise with the history removed by parametric programming; their footnote 4 notes that conditioning on the nuisance component is an over-conditioning that "PP-based SI and other existing SI approaches cannot avoid". All of these treat a single coefficient or a fixed-K group chi statistic with known sigma; none treats an F statistic, a stopping rule together with groups, or a nested pair of selected models.
- Carving in p >> n. Schultheiss, Renaux and Bühlmann (2021, EJS 15(1)) report, in a Toeplitz n = 100, p = 200 design, adjusted power 0.67 for single carving versus 0.73/0.61 for multicarving and state that "carving outperforms splitting with respect to power"; groups are handled by partial F tests under splitting.

### 2.5 The assumption-lean alternative (strong for fixed nulls; not developed for selected nulls)

Rinaldo, Wasserman, G'Sell and Lei (2019, AoS) define the target after selection as the projection parameter of the selected model, prove that "the law of the projection parameter cannot be consistently estimated without sample splitting", and quantify the "inference-prediction tradeoff". Lundborg, Kim, Shah and Samworth (2024, AoS 52(6)) test conditional mean independence with a split-sample projected covariance measure, valid when the product of two regression errors is o_P(n^{-1}), and note in Sec. 3.2 that "given a specified null model, the problem of testing whether X is significant is closely connected to goodness-of-fit testing for the null model"; their Prop. 3 covers high-dimensional Z with the (square-root) lasso. Dhawan, Guo and Shah (2026, arXiv:2607.28861) generalise this to a debiased score test with a "hunt-and-test" split, containing PCM and RP tests as special cases, but the null class F is prespecified; a data-selected F is not discussed. Guo and Shah (2024, JRSS-B) provide rank-transformed subsampling to aggregate over many random splits, with a lack-of-fit example for a linear regression function (Sec. 4.1).

### 2.6 Fisherian model assessment after reduction (new, 2025 to 2026, close to this project)

Battey, García Rasines and Tang (2025) address model adequacy after a lasso or Cox reduction directly. Their co-sufficient statistic Q = U'Y/||U'Y|| is uniform on a sphere under the candidate model, but a single Q "has no power", so they generate k Gaussian-randomised pseudo-replicates (Prop. 5.1, generalising García Rasines and Young) and combine them in a high-dimensional Rayleigh test; sigma^2 is handled by a refitted cross-validation estimator. Two findings matter here. First, "the double use of the data is only problematic if the overfitted E-hat is used as a basis for comparison of fit", which is precisely what M0-versus-M1 does. Second, in their simulations "These high information settings are, however, cases where the split F test dominates the rest", with only "modest advantages ... where the reduction phase is difficult". Battey and Reid (2026, arXiv:2603.27718) recast this as "induced replication" and note that plain co-sufficient tests have "little or no power without modification".

## 3. Where the literature genuinely disagrees

1. Whether to condition at all. Goeman and Solari (2024, Biometrika 111(2):393-416), Observation 1: for any conditional selective procedure with conditional error control there is an unconditional procedure rejecting at least as much (take R' = R and integrate); Props. 1 to 3 give strict inadmissibility, and Prop. 5 says "data splitting and carving are inadmissible in general, at least for FWER control". Their Sec. 13 concedes "We do not have a general recipe for such improvements". Fithian, Sun and Taylor and the sequential-selection papers argue for conditional guarantees as the scientifically relevant ones; Neufeld, Perry and Witten (2026) restate that position. These are different error-rate targets rather than a contradiction, but a reviewer at Biometrika will raise Goeman and Solari against any new conditional test, and the answer must be that the null H0(M0) is itself data-dependent, so the "universe" is the family of all candidate submodels and simultaneous control over it in p > n is not a usable alternative. That argument has to be made, not assumed.
2. Splitting versus carving versus fission. Kivaranovic and Leeb (2024) prove carved or randomised polyhedral intervals are never longer than the corresponding split intervals; Fithian, Sun and Taylor and Schultheiss et al. find carving beats splitting in power; Battey, García Rasines and Tang find the split F test dominates their randomised co-sufficient tests in high-information settings. The results concern different statistics (single contrast versus direction-free omnibus) and are not contradictory, but the ranking for an F-type test of a selected model with unknown sigma is unresolved.
3. Unknown variance. The exact conditional F test handles sigma by conditioning on ||Y|| (Fithian et al. 2015, Loftus and Taylor 2015) and pays in power. The randomised constructions are exact only for known sigma; with a plug-in they are asymptotic (García Rasines and Young Thm 1; Leiner et al. App. B.5, fixed p), and Battey et al. report calibration "compromised in small samples once sigma^2 is estimated". No source gives a finite-sample exact randomised F test with unknown sigma in p > n.

## 4. What is not known (the gaps)

1. No paper analyses a multi-degree-of-freedom block test of a selected model against a selected enlargement. The sequential literature (Fithian et al. 2015; G'Sell et al. 2016; Tibshirani et al. 2016) is single-step; Fithian et al. 2015 test M_{k-1} against the whole upper model but never M_k against M_{k+m}.
2. No power theorem exists for conditional F or chi tests after selection. The Kivaranovic and Leeb mechanism (bounded truncation set) is stated for a scalar contrast; its F-statistic analogue, and the claim that power tends to alpha as the stopping rule binds, is conjecture supported by simulation.
3. Minimal-conditioning computation for the block statistic with groups and a stopping rule does not exist. Sugiyama et al. (Lemma 2, AIC events quadratic in the line parameter) and Shiraishi et al. (Sec. 4.1, group chi test with history removed) supply the pieces, but not the F statistic, not groups plus a stopping rule, and not the nested pair. Two of the delegated readers, independently, expect the gain to be modest because the event "the stopping rule stopped at M0" is itself a quadratic constraint that bounds the statistic near its observed value, and because nuisance-direction conditioning remains. This is an inference, not a result, and it is testable in an afternoon of simulation along the test direction.
4. A randomised or fissioned selective F test with an exact finite-sample guarantee (known sigma) and a uniformly valid studentised version (unknown sigma) has not been written down, and its leftover-information bound (the F/chi analogue of Tian and Taylor Lemma 5) has not been proved.
5. The assumption-lean version, where H0 is that the projection of Y on X_M1 equals its projection on X_M0 and M0, M1 are chosen by a hunt step, is covered by no existing theorem because Dhawan, Guo and Shah, Lundborg et al. and Guo and Shah all prespecify the null class.

## 5. Recommended direction, with its weak points

Recommendation. Write the paper "Goodness-of-fit tests for data-selected linear models: the price of conditioning and how to avoid it" around gaps 1, 2 and 4, with gap 3 as a comparator. Concretely:

1. A negative result. State and prove, for the F (or chi) test of H0(M0) against H1(M0, M1) conditional on a path event of Loftus and Taylor type with a stopping rule, that the conditional power is bounded by a function that tends to alpha as the stopping rule binds, the F analogue of Kivaranovic and Leeb Prop. 1. The stored simulations are the empirical companion. Kivaranovic and Leeb's Remark 3 explicitly leaves this case open.
2. A positive result. A randomised selective F test: select M0 and M1 on U = Y + W, test on V = Y - W/gamma (García Rasines and Young; Leiner et al.), which is an ordinary F test on V with inflated variance and needs no selection-event computation at all; and a carved version that conditions on the U-selection event but uses all of Y, which by Kivaranovic and Leeb (2024) and Fithian, Sun and Taylor should dominate the split-V test. Prove the leftover-information bound for the chi statistic (Tian and Taylor Lemma 5 analogue) and exact validity for known sigma. For unknown sigma, use the García Rasines and Young split-sample sigma-hat or condition on ||V|| and prove uniform asymptotic validity; state honestly that finite-sample exactness is lost.
3. Power theory in the Verzelen and Villers framework: the separation rate the randomised F test achieves against a q-dimensional enlargement, compared with sqrt(q)/n for the oracle F test and k log(p)/n for sparse-directed tests, as a function of gamma. This gives the choice of gamma a theoretical rather than ad hoc basis, which none of the fission papers provide.
4. Benchmarks: the existing fully conditioned test; a minimal-conditioning parametric-programming version (gap 3, to settle whether conditioning on less is enough); the split F test; Battey, García Rasines and Tang's co-sufficient test, which is the nearest published competitor and reports split-F dominance in easy regimes; and a hunt-and-test PCM-type statistic on V as the sparse-directed alternative.

Why this is plausible for JRSS-B or Biometrika. Each ingredient has been published there or in AoS/JASA in the last four years (García Rasines and Young, Biometrika 2023; Kivaranovic and Leeb, JASA 2021 and EJS 2024; Leiner et al., JASA 2025; Goeman and Solari, Biometrika 2024; Lundborg et al., AoS 2024; Guo and Shah, JRSS-B 2024), and the Battey group's 2025 to 2026 preprints show that model adequacy after reduction is an open question at the Fisherian end too. The package already contains the fully conditioned test, the simulation harness and the RP comparison, so the empirical section is largely built.

Weak points, in decreasing order of risk.

- The negative result may be regarded as folklore. Fithian, Sun and Taylor's eq. 21 and Kivaranovic and Leeb's Prop. 1 already contain the idea. It counts only if stated as a theorem about the F test with a stopping rule and proved; otherwise it is a remark.
- Goeman and Solari's Observation 1 says an unconditional procedure at least as good always exists. If a referee constructs one for this problem (for example a split F test with Fisher combination over splits, which they call "a natural and more powerful choice" than carving), the conditional machinery loses its point. The paper needs a direct comparison against that construction and a clear statement of what the conditional guarantee buys.
- Battey, García Rasines and Tang find the split F test dominates in high-information settings. If the randomised F test does not beat the split F test by a margin that matters, the contribution reduces to the negative result and the power theory.
- Unknown sigma. Biometrika will want at least an exact known-sigma theorem plus uniform asymptotics; a plug-in with only pointwise asymptotics will be criticised, and Leiner et al. say the conditional distribution given sigma-hat "may not have a tractable distribution in high dimensional settings".
- The alternative M1 is unusual. Reviewers may ask why the enlargement is the next m steps of the same path rather than all unselected variables (impossible when p > n) or a sparse-directed hunt. Offering the hunt-and-test statistic on V as a second alternative answers this, but makes the paper longer.
- The minimal-conditioning comparator (gap 3) might, contrary to the readers' expectation, recover most of the power. That would be a good outcome scientifically but would move the paper's centre of gravity toward a computational contribution that has so far appeared in machine-learning venues (ICML, AISTATS, JJSDS), not in statistics journals.

Secondary option. An assumption-lean paper extending Dhawan, Guo and Shah's hunt-and-test to a data-selected null class, targeting projection parameters as in Rinaldo et al. It fits the JRSS-B Shah-group programme, but the risk that it is judged an application of their theorems is high, and it discards the package's selective F machinery.

Not recommended. Further work on the RP test applied to lasso-added variables (invalid by the Janková et al. mechanism); a parametric-programming implementation alone (incremental over Sugiyama et al. and Shiraishi et al.).
